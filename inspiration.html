I've been messing around with HTML and CSS for over ten years now as well as very mild video game modding (and modding mods!) and involvement with FOSS communities, Linux more recently. I write, on and off, and lately have been taking up Blender; you'll probably notice I tend to pick skills that drive people to the brink of insanity.

There's nothing of major interest to show off at the moment, but in the future I'd like to use all of these towards reviving in-browser interactive fiction with point and click elements. Old point and click games - especially the weirder ones like The Dark Eye and Planet Laika - have always had a pretty major foothold in my brain. With how accessible JS is these days, there's no reason why these can't see some kind of renaissance. Not only that, but despite the popularity of virtual pet games back in the day and people frequently saying they wish Neopets would come back, there isn't really much out there at the moment. Unless you want to go down the mobile free to play route. And why not use the basis of point and click interactive fiction and virtual pets for more practical purposes? There's absolutely grounds for creating museum-like resources online, especially in the age of AI misinformation and perpetual lack of public interest in scientific fields.


Immersive Bags by MIU MIU


Although MIU MIU is decidedly not centered around fake pets, informative material, or interactive fiction, this website displays pretty much exactly what I'm aiming for. It's pretty interesting a company decided to launch a marketing campaign like this for designer bags. It's much more interesting than sending an influencer free things. I hope, despite not caring about this, this creativity is adopted by other brands.

Upon entering the site itself, an extremely short cutscene plays. Once it finishes, it seamlessly switches to a point and click scene with fixed cameras. This method stood out to me because it's reminiscent of how older games used sets a character would walk around in (rather than the environment changing external of the players point of view). Personally I found the wobbly camera to be distracting. I liked that clicking on the notebook itself would bring up the item that would be used throughout the game. I really do wonder if whoever was hired to do this had experience in game design.

Once finishing the first task, the user is sent further on to explore the house. Which again, uses the point and click element, along with the classic navigation based on entrance interactions. Though not so much on the pet end of the spectrum, this format would be perfect for games and digital museums.
